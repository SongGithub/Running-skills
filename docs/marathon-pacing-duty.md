# Marathon Pacing Duty

How to prepare for and execute an official pacemaker duty at marathon distance.

A pacing duty is **not a race**. The deliverable is a specific finish time, evenly
distributed, with the group intact. Personal ambition is out of scope.

## 1. Convert the target to a pace and split table

Target time T over 42.195 km:

    pace = T / 42.195

Produce cumulative splits before the event and write them on your arm or a card.
Do not compute during the race.

| Split | Formula |
|---|---|
| Each 5 km | pace x 5 |
| Half | T / 2 |
| 30 km | pace x 30 |
| Finish | T |

**Even splits are the default.** A slight negative split (second half a few seconds per
km faster) is safer physiologically than going out fast, but the group expectation is
usually even. Confirm the policy with the race director.

## 2. Readiness gate - run this check before accepting the duty

The binding constraint for a pacemaker is almost never aerobic fitness. It is
**musculoskeletal durability and fuelling**, and neither adapts quickly.

Ask three questions:

1. **Longest run in the last 8 weeks vs race distance.** If the longest recent run is
   less than roughly 65-70 percent of race distance, the jump is a real injury risk.
2. **Weeks available.** Distance durability needs at least one long run that
   approximates the demand. If there is no room for that session, the duty is at risk.
3. **Target pace vs current easy pace.** If the target pace sits at the *top* of
   aerobic zone rather than inside easy range, four hours of it is a genuine effort,
   not a recovery jog. Budget accordingly.

If any answer is unfavourable, tell the organisers **early**. They can reassign more
easily than an athlete can recover from a blow-up or an injury.

## 3. Preparation block structure

Typically 2-4 weeks from a prior race. Priorities in order:

| Priority | Session | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Long run at target pace for 60-70 percent of race distance | Pace rehearsal, fuelling, kit check |
| 2 | Long run with a paced block in the back half | Practise holding pace when fatigued |
| 3 | Short pace confirmation run in race week | Reassure the legs, do not fatigue them |
| - | **No intervals, no threshold work** | Not the limiter; adds cost without benefit |

Suggested three-week shape when starting from a recent race:

- **Week 1** - recover from the prior race; one long run at ~55 percent of race
  distance, with the final third at target pace
- **Week 2** - the key week; one long run at ~70-75 percent of race distance, with
  60-70 percent of it at exact target pace, full race kit and fuelling
- **Week 3** - taper; volume down 40 percent, one short run containing a paced block

## 4. Fuelling

A four-hour effort cannot be fuelled on feel. Rehearse it in the key long run, exactly
as planned for race day.

- **Carbohydrate:** 60-90 g/h, starting 30-45 min in, not when hungry
- **Fluid:** 500-750 ml/h depending on conditions
- **Nothing new on race day** - no new shoes, gels, or clothing

## 5. Course and conditions

- **Undulating courses:** hold effort and heart rate, let pace float. Forcing even pace
  up a climb costs far more than the seconds it saves. Tell the group this in advance.
- **Gravel or technical surfaces:** expect a slower pace for the same effort; recalibrate
  expectations rather than the effort.
- **Heat:** apply a pace penalty per 5 C above approximately 12-15 C. Never chase pace
  and heart rate simultaneously - protect the effort and let the pace go.
- **Wind and rain:** reduce ambition rather than increase effort.

## 6. Race-day execution

1. **Confirm gun time vs chip time.** Pacers are usually judged on gun time. Start at
   the front of the correct group.
2. **Hold back for the first 5 km.** Group energy pushes early pace. Banking time is a
   loan at high interest, repaid at 30 km.
3. **Pace by effort on gradients**, by watch on the flat.
4. **Fuel on schedule**, independent of how you feel.
5. **Communication is part of the job.** Announce the pace and the split at each marker.
6. **Finishing slightly late is a success.** Blowing up and walking is not.

## 7. Pre-event checklist

- [ ] Target time confirmed in writing, and gun vs chip clarified
- [ ] Split table written and carried
- [ ] Course profile obtained; pacing strategy adjusted
- [ ] Key long run completed at target pace with race fuelling
- [ ] Race kit and shoes proven in training
- [ ] Weather forecast checked; pace penalty decided in advance
- [ ] Bail-out criteria agreed with yourself before the start
